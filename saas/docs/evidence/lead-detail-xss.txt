--- what this shows ---
Three hostile strings reach the DOM through three different paths — the company
name (lead field), an evidence quote (model output echoing attacker text), and
the notes body — and all three render as visible text. Browser console after
loading this page contained no dialog and no error other than React's
development-mode eval() notice.

The escaping is React's. The second layer is the Content-Security-Policy in
next.config.ts, which forbids object-src, frame-ancestors and external script
origins, so a future templating mistake in one of these fields does not become
script execution.

Note also what the model did here: it quoted the hostile span verbatim, which is
correct. A quote must be what the source actually said. Sanitising evidence at
capture time would corrupt the audit trail; it is rendered inert at display time
instead.
